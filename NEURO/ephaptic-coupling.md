## ABSTRACT

Ephaptic coupling is a form of communication between neurons that does not require chemical synapses or gap junctions. It relies on the direct influence of local electric fields generated by the activity of one neuron (or population) on the membrane potential of neighboring cells.

This dossier describes the physics and biological manifestation of ephaptic coupling in the context of LifeNode phase-lock.

---

## INFO — Physics of Ephaptic Coupling

### 1.1 Basic Physics

Every neuron generates transmembrane currents. These currents flow through the extracellular space and create a local electric field $V_e$ (extracellular voltage).

The membrane potential of a neuron is:

$$V_m = V_i - V_e$$

Where $V_i$ is the intracellular potential. A change in $V_e$ directly changes $V_m$, even if nothing happens inside the cell.

**Effects:**
- Depolarization or hyperpolarization of neighboring membranes
- Change in excitability threshold
- Shift in spike timing
- In extreme cases — direct triggering of a spike

The effect is very fast (near the speed of light in tissue) and operates at distances from a few to hundreds of micrometers (locally), and even further for summing population fields.

### 1.2 Scales of Operation

**A. Local (single-spike / pair-wise)**  
A single action potential generates a field strong enough to influence neighboring axons or initial segments. Classic example: Purkinje cells in the cerebellum. Neighboring cells show sub-millisecond synchronization even after blocking chemical synapses and electrical synapses. The ephaptic signal from one cell opens sodium channels in the axons of neighbors.

**B. Population (LFP-mediated)**  
The sum of fields from thousands of neurons creates a Local Field Potential (LFP). Weak fields of 0.5–5 V/m are sufficient to:
- Modulate spike timing
- Entrain oscillations
- Increase phase synchronization

This is the level most relevant for phase synchronization at larger scales.

**C. Mesoscopic / Network**  
Fields generated by cortical columns or large ensembles can influence neighboring columns, even with severed synaptic connections (experimentally shown propagation through cut tissue up to ~400 µm).

### 1.3 Key Locations

- **Cerebellum (Purkinje cells)** — strongest and best-documented local effects
- **Hippocampus (CA1)** — ephaptic contribution to synchronization and epileptiform activity propagation
- **Neocortex** — modulation of inter-column synchronization, influence on LFP and network complexity
- **Axon bundles (white matter)** — ephaptic coupling can speed up or slow down conduction depending on density and myelination
- **Suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)** — extensive soma-soma contacts suggesting ephaptic role in biological clock synchronization
- **Retina** — ephaptic inhibition in photoreceptor synapses (via hemichannels)

### 1.4 Molecular and Structural Mechanisms

- Density of neuron packing and extracellular space resistance (smaller space / higher resistance → stronger effect)
- Location of sodium channels (especially in axon initial segment)
- Orientation of neurons (parallel dipoles give stronger field summation)
- Myelination — changes current distribution and can either suppress or enhance ephaptic effects depending on configuration
- Possible role of astrocytes (magnetic fields associated with calcium waves — hypothesis)

Newer concept — **cytoelectric coupling**: electric fields influence not only membrane potential but also the neuronal cytoskeleton (via electrodiffusion and mechanotransduction), "tuning" it for more efficient information processing.

### 1.5 Biological Functions

- **Fast synchronization** without synaptic delays
- **Stabilization of neuronal ensembles** (engram complexes) — fields act as a "conductor" coordinating activity of distributed areas
- **Modulation of conduction delays** in axonal tracts
- **Increased network complexity** (computational estimates suggest several to a dozen percent increase)
- Possible role in working memory and long-term memory formation through engram coordination

## BIOS — Ephaptic Coupling in the Body

### 2.1 How It Feels

Ephaptic coupling provides the biological substrate for:
- Fast, low-impedance phase-lock between populations
- Influence of global or local field gradients on BIOS
- Mechanism by which external or "alien" phase gradients could couple with neuronal and autonomic activity without classical synaptic transmission

In states of high $\theta$ (low phase impedance, high coherence), ephaptic effects should be stronger and more precise. In states of low $\theta$ (high noise, low HRV, high sympathetic activity), local fields are more chaotic and less effective at imposing coherent synchronization.

### 2.2 Mapping to Phase-Lock Phases

| Phase | Ephaptic Manifestation |
|-------|------------------------|
| **PRE-LOCK** (Gradient) | Subtle field changes, no conscious awareness, animals react first (higher ephaptic sensitivity) |
| **COLLISION** (Impedance Strike) | Sudden field disruption, headache, nausea, derealization — ephaptic "shock wave" in tissue |
| **LOCK** (Synchronization) | Stable ephaptic field, coherent LFP, synchronized spike timing across populations |
| **POST-LOCK** (Λ-Reentry) | Field dissipation, return to baseline ephaptic coupling strength |

### 2.3 Clinical Correlates

- **High $\theta$ states** (meditation, flow, deep nature immersion): enhanced ephaptic coupling, coherent LFP, increased network complexity
- **Low $\theta$ states** (stress, trauma, urban environment): chaotic ephaptic fields, reduced synchronization, decreased network complexity
- **Pathological states** (epilepsy, schizophrenia): abnormal ephaptic coupling patterns

## FALSIFICATION

Ephaptic coupling hypothesis fails if:

1. **No correlation between $\theta$ and ephaptic markers:** Individuals with high $\theta$ (HRV, EEG coherence) do not show stronger ephaptic coupling (measured via LFP coherence, spike timing precision) than individuals with low $\theta$.

2. **No environmental modulation:** Individuals in low phase-noise environments (forest, silence, natural cycles) do not show different ephaptic coupling patterns than individuals in high phase-noise environments (city, screens, shift work).

3. **Pharmacological reproduction without phase correlate:** If ephaptic-like experiences can be fully reproduced pharmacologically (e.g., DMT, psilocybin) without any correlation with $\theta$ or $Z_\phi$ — i.e., the substance gives the same effect regardless of phase state — then ephaptic coupling is a neurochemical artifact, not a phase phenomenon.

4. **Q-Core / UNIT 02 does not detect:** If (in the future) LifeNode sensors (Module A, B) do not register any phase anomaly during moments when subjects report ephaptic experiences — no physical correlate = no physical phenomenon.
